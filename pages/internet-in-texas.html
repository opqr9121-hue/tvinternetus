Internet & TV Providers in Texas: 2026 Availability Guide

Texas has one of the widest broadband gaps of any large state — dense fiber coverage in Austin, Dallas-Fort Worth, Houston, and San Antonio sits next to large rural stretches still served only by satellite or fixed wireless. What's actually available at your address depends heavily on which part of the state you're in, so we break it down by region below instead of giving one statewide answer.

Texas Broadband Snapshot

  • Dominant technologies: Cable (Spectrum, Xfinity) and DSL/fiber (AT&T) cover most metro areas; fixed wireless and satellite fill rural gaps.
  • Fiber leaders: AT&T Fiber (largest footprint), Google Fiber (Austin metro), Frontier Fiber (parts of Dallas-Fort Worth, Houston suburbs).
  • Rural coverage: HughesNet, Viasat, and Starlink are often the only options outside metro and suburban corridors.
  • State oversight: The Texas Comptroller's Broadband Development Office maintains its own state map, built after the Comptroller's office publicly flagged the FCC's federal map as overstating coverage in parts of the state.

Sources: FCC National Broadband Map · Texas Broadband Development Office

TV & Streaming Options in Texas

Alongside internet, most Texas households can also get DIRECTV STREAM regardless of which internet provider serves their address — it runs over any broadband connection, which makes it a common pairing in areas where cable TV bundles aren't available (much of rural and West Texas). Frequently Asked Questions — Texas Internet

Is fiber internet available across Texas?

No — fiber is concentrated in and around major metros (Austin, Dallas-Fort Worth, Houston, San Antonio). Most rural areas rely on fixed wireless or satellite.

What's the cheapest reliable internet option in rural Texas?

T-Mobile or Verizon 5G Home Internet, where available, is typically cheaper than satellite and doesn't carry data caps the way older satellite plans do.

Is Texas getting federal funding to expand broadband?

Yes — Texas is one of the largest recipients of BEAD (Broadband Equity, Access, and Deployment) funding, administered through the Texas Broadband Development Office, aimed at closing gaps in unserved and underserved rural areas over the next several years.
