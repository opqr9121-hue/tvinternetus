How to choose the right provider

The best internet provider for you depends on three things: what's actually available at your address, how you use the internet, and your budget. Start by checking your ZIP code on our homepage — availability varies block by block, not just city by city.

Once you know who's available, compare these factors:

  • Technology type — Fiber is fastest and most reliable; cable is widely available; 5G home internet is easy to set up; DSL works in rural areas.
  • Download & upload speeds — Streaming and browsing need 25–100 Mbps. Remote work and gaming benefit from 100+ Mbps with good upload speeds.
  • Price after promo period — Many providers offer low intro rates that increase after 12 months. Check the regular price before signing up.
  • Data caps & contracts — Most major providers now offer unlimited data, but always confirm. Avoid contracts if you might move soon.

What speed do you actually need?

More speed isn't always better — it usually means a higher bill. Here's a practical guide:

  • 25–50 Mbps — 1–2 users, email, browsing, SD streaming
  • 100 Mbps — Small household, HD streaming on 2–3 devices
  • 300 Mbps — Family of 4, multiple streams, video calls
  • 500 Mbps – 1 Gig — Heavy gamers, 4K streaming, smart home devices
  • 2 Gig+ — Power users, large uploads, multiple simultaneous 4K streams

Fiber vs. Cable vs. 5G

Fiber uses light signals through glass cables. It offers symmetrical upload and download speeds, low latency, and excellent reliability. If fiber is available at your address, it's almost always the best choice.

Cable uses coaxial lines originally built for TV. It's widely available and fast for downloads, but upload speeds are typically much slower. Good for most households that don't upload large files regularly.

5G Home Internet uses cellular towers to deliver broadband wirelessly. Setup is simple (plug in a gateway, no technician visit), but speeds vary by signal strength at your location.
